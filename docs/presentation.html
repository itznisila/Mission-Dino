01 · The challenge

Every kilogram costs something

The problem

  • Mission design is a web of trade-offs: mass, power, data, radiation and money all pull against each other.
  • The public sees launches and pretty pictures, not the decisions that make them possible.
  • Textbooks explain each system alone, never how one choice ripples through the rest.

Our answer

  • A game where you design a real-style mission from idea to operations.
  • Simplified but genuine physics, so choices behave like they do for NASA engineers.
  • Wrapped in a space exploration site built from real NASA imagery and data.

03 · Real engineering, simplified

The physics is real

Rocket equation

Tsiolkovsky: every extra kilogram of hardware needs extra fuel, which needs a bigger rocket.

Sunlight

Falls with distance squared: solar panels make 43% of their Earth power at Mars, under 4% at Jupiter.

Data link

Farther targets mean a weaker signal. Collect more than you can send and the rest is lost.

Radiation

Jupiter's belts wear out electronics. Shielding and hardened computers buy lifetime.

04 · Every decision matters

Run the mission, live

  • Random events: solar storms, wheel failures, ground station outages, budget cuts.
  • Each option shows what is at stake. Your design decides the damage.
  • Budget, science and lifetime projections update after every choice.
  • Full success, partial success or failure, from the real results.
  • Team role goals reward negotiation: +50 points each.
  • Compared with Landsat 9, Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter and Juno.

06 · Impact

Think like a mission team

Who it's for

  • Students and classrooms
  • Space fans curious about how missions work
  • Groups of up to 4 on one screen

What's creative

  • Roles with clashing goals force real negotiation
  • Shareable designs as a link
  • Failure is a lesson, not a game over

How it's built

  • One HTML file, plain JavaScript, no install
  • Responsive on phone, tablet and desktop
  • Automated self-check in Node.js
